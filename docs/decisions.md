# The decisions of the release

[spec.md](spec.md) is normative for the behavior. This file records the
decisions behind it: what was decided, why, and where the evidence is. A
decision that changes the contract is recorded in the spec and in the
changelog, never only in the code.

The decisions that shape the contract are in [spec.md](spec.md) §15; the
two lists are numbered independently.

| # | Question | Decision | Status | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The supported runtime versions | The gateway is built, tested and released on **Bun 1.4.3**; the contract states **Bun ≥ 1.4.3**. The 1.2 line was measured (1.2.0 and 1.2.23): it does not expose the early answer while the upload runs, it does not cancel the producer of the body, the `ws` client cannot observe the refusal of an upstream handshake, and the upgrade of the caller with the headers of the gateway is refused. | resolved | [runtime.md](runtime.md), `test/network/transport.test.ts` |
| 2 | The upload and the response commitment boundaries | The observable events are the **end of the request stream**, the **final response headers** and the **first byte of the body**. The acceptance of the bytes by the peer is not observable and is not claimed. A failure after the headers are sent ends the body instead of replacing the answer. | resolved | `test/network/transport.test.ts` |
| 3 | The refusal of an upstream WebSocket handshake | It is reported apart from a network failure: the caller receives `502 bad_gateway`, the body of the upstream is not forwarded, the status of the refusal is in the log, and the slot of the connection is released. | resolved | `test/network/transport.test.ts` |
| 4 | The establishment of the connection during the upload | No extra ceiling is added. The upload is governed by the idle `bodyMs` budget only: a client that keeps making progress is never cut off by a total budget. The establishment of the connection to the upstream is part of the `responseMs` budget. | resolved | `test/network/transport.test.ts` |
| 5 | The `Authorization` header of the caller and the standard `Forwarded` header | The standard `forwarded` header is **removed** in both directions. The `Authorization` header of the caller is **forwarded** to the upstream unchanged: the upstream is an application of the same platform that the operator chose, and the identity of the caller travels in the headers of the gateway and in the signature. | resolved | `src/domains/forwarding/headers.test.ts` |
| 6 | The representation of the headers | The roles are written as a comma separated list. A role that contains a comma or a control character is dropped from the header and stays in the request to the access service. The identity values are truncated to 512 characters and the control characters are removed. The name of a cookie is case sensitive. | resolved | `src/domains/forwarding/identity-signature.test.ts`, `src/domains/forwarding/headers.test.ts` |
| 7 | The purpose of a token | The provider does not publish a token type claim, so the gateway validates the signature, the issuer, the audience, the expiry and the subject, and nothing else. The algorithms are pinned and the tolerance of the clock is five seconds. A deployment whose provider publishes a token type claim adds the check in the authentication domain; the ports make it a local change. The **type** of the token is a separate rule, and it is decision 38: a bearer token that carries a nonce is refused, whatever the claims of the provider. | resolved | `test/integration/oidc.test.ts` |
| 8 | The consumption of the login transaction | The state of the login is single use on four levels: the state cookie is deleted as soon as the callback is processed, the state of the callback has to be the state of the cookie, the verifier and the nonce bind the exchange to the login attempt, and the authorization code is single use at the provider. Two replicas that process the same callback at the same time both reach the provider, and the provider refuses the second exchange. | resolved | `src/domains/authentication/principal.test.ts` |
| 9 | The outage of the cache of the decisions | Only an explicit decision of the access service is cached. A transport failure is never cached. When the cache is unavailable the gateway asks the service again and denies when that fails. | resolved | `src/domains/workspace-access/workspace-access.test.ts`, `test/integration/access.test.ts` |
| 10 | The live authorization of a WebSocket | The connection is authorized at the handshake **and** revalidated every `SHUTTLE_WS_REVALIDATE_INTERVAL_MS`. The revalidation checks the route, the session and the access, asks the access service again instead of reading the cache, and closes the connection with the code `1008` when one of them fails. A revalidation that cannot be made closes the connection: a connection the gateway cannot vouch for is a connection it must not keep. | resolved | `src/domains/websockets/bridge.test.ts`, `test/contract/websocket-revocation.test.ts` |
| 11 | The provisioning of the verifier of the signature | The upstream is given the **derived** key, not the master secret. The key is versioned by the version of the signature (`v3`). | resolved | `src/domains/forwarding/identity-signature.test.ts` |
| 12 | The external host and the caching of the responses | The gateway does not cache a response and does not add cache headers. The answers of the upstreams are forwarded as they are, including their cache headers: the gateway is not the owner of the policy of the caching. | resolved | `test/contract/endpoints.test.ts` |
| 13 | The topology of the replicas and the TLS of the shared state | The replicas share the sessions, the rate limits and the cache of the decisions through a server whose state is shared. The TLS of the endpoint is verified with the certificate authority of the deployment. The caps of a process (sessions, connections, buffers, exchanges) are per process: a deployment with several replicas multiplies them. | resolved | [operations.md](operations.md), `src/runtime/env.ts` |
| 14 | The drain policy | During the drain the readiness probe answers `503` with the body `draining`, the listener stops accepting new work, the WebSocket connections are closed with the code 1001, and the exchanges that are running are cancelled when the deadline is reached. | resolved | `test/contract/lifecycle.test.ts` |
| 15 | The host of a request | The **effective host** is the `x-forwarded-host` of a trusted proxy and the `Host` header otherwise. It decides the route, the origin the browser is compared with, and the value the signature covers. The header of a client that is not a trusted proxy is ignored, and a value that is not a plausible host is ignored. The last value of the list is used, because a proxy that appends writes its own value last. | resolved | `test/contract/forwarding.test.ts` |
| 16 | The metrics of the requests | Every answer of the gateway is counted, the refusals included, with the route it was matched on or `unmatched`. The labels are bounded: `other` for a method or a status that is not tracked, so neither a client nor an upstream can grow the number of series without limit. The route is capped at 500, with the rest aggregated into `overflow`. | resolved | `test/contract/metrics.test.ts`, `src/observability/observability.test.ts` |
| 17 | The scopes of the limits | Four scopes: the deployment, the caller over all the routes, the caller on one route, and the authenticated caller on one route. A scope keyed by an **address** cannot bound a flood that spreads over many addresses, and it cannot tell two tenants behind the same address apart. The scope of the deployment has no address in it; the scope of a principal is keyed by the subject. | resolved | `src/domains/rate-limiting/rate-limiting.test.ts` |
| 18 | The table of the limiter of the memory | The table of the windows is bounded at 10 000 keys, and a key leaves it when its window is over, **never** because another key needed its place. A limiter that evicts a live window hands the evicted caller a fresh budget, so a caller that sprays addresses resets its own counter at will. When the table is full, the requests that do not fit are counted in one shared window, so the flood is bounded by the same limit. | resolved | `src/domains/rate-limiting/rate-limiting.test.ts` |
| 19 | The number of exchanges at the same time | The exchanges are bounded by `SHUTTLE_MAX_CONCURRENT_REQUESTS`. A client that opens a request and never sends its body holds a connection until the idle budget of the upload runs out, which is five minutes by default; without a cap, that is enough to exhaust the resources of the process. An exchange that does not fit is refused with `503 gateway_overloaded` rather than queued. | resolved | `src/domains/proxying/exchange.test.ts` |
| 20 | The rate limits of a production deployment | The gateway refuses to start in production when every limit is disabled, unless `SHUTTLE_ALLOW_NO_RATE_LIMITS=true`. The limiter is the only protection of the gateway against a flood, and a setting that looks active but is not a security hole. | resolved | `src/runtime/env.test.ts` |
| 21 | The revocation of an access | The latency of a revocation is the TTL of the cache of the decisions (`SHUTTLE_ACCESS_CACHE_TTL_MS`, 5 s by default). It is the knob an operator turns when a revocation has to be immediate. The revalidation of a WebSocket asks the service again and does not read the cache. | resolved | `src/domains/workspace-access/workspace-access.test.ts` |
| 22 | The state of a production deployment | The state is shared: the sessions and the state of the replicas live in a Redis server or in a Postgres server, or the deployment says explicitly that it is a single node that keeps them in the process (`SHUTTLE_ALLOW_MEMORY_STATE=true`). A session in the memory of one replica is unknown to the others, so the callers are logged out at random, and a limit in the memory of one replica is the limit of that replica and not the limit of the deployment, so a flood is bounded by the number of the replicas. | resolved | `src/runtime/env.test.ts` |
| 23 | The policy of the cross site requests of a route | It is a property of the route (`crossSite`). A navigation from another site is allowed by default, because refusing it breaks every deep link into an application; a route whose upstream changes the state of something on a `GET` declares `strict` and is only reached from the site itself. The policy of the deployment (`SHUTTLE_CSRF_STRICT`) applies to the routes that declare none. | resolved | `src/domains/configuration/configuration.test.ts`, `test/contract/policies.test.ts` |
| 24 | The `Authorization` header of the caller | It is forwarded to the upstream by default: the upstream is an application of the same platform that the operator chose. A route that does not need it declares `forwardAuthorization: false` and does not receive it: the header is a credential of the caller, and forwarding it places the upstream in the trust domain of that credential. The identity of the caller travels in the headers of the gateway and in the signature either way. | resolved | `src/domains/forwarding/headers.test.ts`, `test/contract/policies.test.ts` |
| 25 | The latency of a revocation | It is a property of the route (`accessCacheTtlMs`). The lifetime of the environment is the default of the routes that declare none; a route that declares `0` asks the access service for every request, so a revocation on that route is immediate. The cache key holds the route, so the decisions of two routes never mix. | resolved | `src/domains/workspace-access/workspace-access.test.ts`, `test/contract/policies.test.ts` |
| 26 | The header policy of the handshake of a websocket | The handshake is a request to the upstream: it follows the header policy of the http path. The cookies of the gateway never reach an upstream, the identity of the caller is written by the gateway, and the headers of the handshake of the caller are not forwarded. Before this release the handshake copied the headers of the caller, and the session of the caller reached the upstream. | resolved | `src/domains/forwarding/headers.test.ts`, `test/contract/policies.test.ts` |
| 27 | The TLS of the connections to the upstreams | The gateway verifies the certificate of the upstream with the authority of the deployment and presents the certificate of the deployment when the upstream asks for one. The certificate of the gateway and its private key belong together: a deployment that sets one without the other is refused at the start, because a setting that looks active but is not a security hole. The upstream of a websocket is served over plain http: the websocket client of the runtime cannot speak TLS. | resolved | `src/runtime/env.test.ts`, `test/network/tls.test.ts` |
| 28 | The format of the signature of the identity | The signature is a **JWS** (RFC 7515) signed with `HS256` and the derived key, with a **detached** payload. The payload is a canonical JSON object with named fields. The upstream rebuilds the payload from the request and from the identity headers, rebuilds the compact serialization around it and verifies it with a standard library. The format of `v3` was a hex digest of a canonical JSON array: it forced every upstream to implement a signature of Shuttle and to compare a value, and a comparison can be written wrong. The detached payload turns the verification into one call of a library, and the object turns the reconstruction into a list of fields that cannot be confused. | resolved | `src/domains/forwarding/identity-signature.test.ts` |
| 29 | The parsing of the cookies | The `Cookie` and the `Set-Cookie` headers are parsed and rendered by the library that implements the standard. The gateway had two parsers of its own, with two slightly different rules: a quoted value, a value that carries an `=` and an `Expires` that carries a comma were not handled the way RFC 6265 describes them. The first value of a name that appears twice is kept, because a browser sends the cookie of the longest path first. | resolved | `src/domains/forwarding/cookies.test.ts` |
| 30 | The window of the limiters | The window **slides**: the count of a caller is the number of its requests in the last `windowMs` milliseconds. A fixed window lets a caller spend its whole budget at the end of a window and again at the beginning of the next one, so twice the limit crosses the gateway in a moment. The window is counted in buckets of at most 60, so the state of a caller does not grow with its traffic, and the boundary of a bucket is a moment every replica can compute, so the replicas count in the same buckets. | resolved | `src/domains/rate-limiting/window.test.ts`, `test/integration/redis-limiter.test.ts` |
| 31 | The client of the shared state | The client of Redis is the one of the `redis` package, and not the one of the runtime. The shared state is the part of the deployment that has to survive a change of runtime, and the client of the runtime could not speak to a cluster nor to a sentinel. The offline queue stays disabled: a command that is sent before the connection is established fails right away, so the gateway denies the caller instead of piling up the requests. The connection is established in the background, so a gateway that starts while the shared state is down stays up and becomes ready when the server answers. | resolved | `src/platform/redis/client.test.ts`, `test/integration/redis-sessions.test.ts` |
| 32 | The backend of the shared state | The state of the replicas lives in a **Redis server or in a Postgres server**: the scheme of the url picks the backend, and the two hold the same three things with the same contract. The contract is the same because the same suite of tests runs against both (`test/integration/shared-state-conformance.ts`), and the measurements of the two are side by side (`scripts/benchmark.ts`). The choice is not made by the gateway: it is made by the deployment, and the gateway measures both so that the choice is an informed one. | resolved | `test/integration/postgres-conformance.test.ts`, `test/integration/redis-conformance.test.ts`, [capacity.md](capacity.md) |
| 33 | The atomicity of the shared state in Postgres | The two scripts of Redis become two **functions** of Postgres: a function call is a transaction of the server, so it is the equivalent of a script. The checks of one key are serialized with an advisory lock, so two replicas that count the same request at the same time count it once: the counter of the deployment is exact, the test of the replicas proves it with several processes. | resolved | `src/platform/postgres/schema.ts`, `test/integration/postgres-replicas.test.ts` |
| 34 | The durability of the shared state in Postgres | The server does **not** wait for the disk (`SHUTTLE_POSTGRES_SYNCHRONOUS_COMMIT=off`, the default): the state of the shared state is a protection and not a ledger, and the guarantee is the one a Redis server gives without its append only file. It is the difference between a millisecond and a tenth of a millisecond on every transaction of the limiter, and the limiter is on the path of every request. | resolved | [capacity.md](capacity.md), `src/platform/postgres/client.ts` |
| 35 | The expiry of the rows of Postgres | The expiry of a key of Redis is the key that disappears; the expiry of a row is a column. The rows that are over are removed by a sweep that the adapter runs, and the server never serves a row that is over whatever the sweep did: the expiry is checked on the read, not only by the sweep. | resolved | `test/integration/postgres-conformance.test.ts` |
| 36 | The version of the runtime | The gateway **refuses to start** on a runtime older than **Bun 1.4.3**. The contract states the version of the runtime, but nothing checked it: a gateway started on the 1.2 line booted happily and then behaved in ways the specification does not describe — the early answer of an upstream was not delivered, the producer of the body was not cancelled, the refusal of an upstream websocket handshake was not observable and the upgrade of the caller with the headers of the gateway was refused. Those differences are only visible in production, so the version is checked before the process reads its environment. | resolved | `src/runtime/version.ts`, `src/runtime/version.test.ts` |
| 37 | An outage of the access service | The gateway fails closed in both cases, but it does not **answer** the same way. An explicit `{"allow": false}` is a denial: `403 workspace_forbidden`, counted as `access_denied`. A service that could not be asked, or an answer that could not be read, is the **unavailability of the service**: `503 access_unavailable` with a `retry-after`, counted on `shuttle_access_unavailable_total`. The refusal was the same before, and it hid the outage: an operator could not tell a failing access service from a policy that denies the callers, and the metric of the denials counted the outage as a denial. | resolved | `src/domains/workspace-access/decision.ts`, `src/app/dispatcher.ts`, `test/contract/endpoints.test.ts` |
| 38 | The type of a bearer token | A bearer token has to be an **access token**. The gateway refused an id token as well, because it validated the signature, the issuer, the audience, the expiry and the subject and nothing else, and many providers publish an id token and an access token for the same client and the same audience. An id token travels through the browser: it lands in the history, in the logs and in the referrers, and accepting one hands a credential that was meant for the browser to every route that accepts a bearer token. The browser flow of the gateway always asks for a nonce, so the id token of this client always carries a `nonce` claim and an access token never does: a bearer token that carries a nonce is refused. A provider that publishes a claim that names the type of the token names it in `SHUTTLE_OIDC_REQUIRED_TOKEN_CLAIMS`. The check of the nonce is on unless the deployment turns it off. | resolved | `src/domains/authentication/oidc.ts`, `test/integration/oidc.test.ts`, `test/contract/endpoints.test.ts` |
| 39 | The budgets of a request | The three budgets of a request — the deployment, the caller over all the routes and the caller on the route — are counted **at the same time**. They are independent, so there is no reason to wait for one before asking for the next one, and the state the replicas share is asked once instead of three times. The measurements of the capacity attribute the cost of the shared state to the number of round trips, and this one is on the path of every request. The refusal that is reported is the first of the order of the specification, and **every budget is counted** even when one of them refuses: the request was made, and the budget of the caller is spent by the requests that were made. The order of the pipeline is not changed: the budgets are counted before the authentication, so a flood is stopped before it reaches the session store. | resolved | `src/app/limits.ts`, `src/app/limits.test.ts`, `test/integration/redis-round-trips.test.ts` |
| 40 | The key of the identity signature | The signature of the identity is **asymmetric** in a production deployment: the gateway holds the private key of the deployment and the upstreams hold the **public** key, so an upstream that verifies an identity cannot mint one. The symmetric format gave every upstream the secret that signs the identity, so the trust boundary was the network and not the key: an upstream that could reach the gateway could also forge an identity for any subject. The key is an elliptic curve key on the curve P-256 and the signature is an `ES256` one — a JWS, so an upstream verifies it with a standard library. The gateway refuses to start in production without the key: a setting that looks active but is not is a security hole. The version of the format is `v5`, so a signature of the symmetric format never verifies with the public key and the other way round. | resolved | `src/domains/forwarding/identity.ts`, `scripts/identity-key.ts`, `test/contract/identity-signature.test.ts` |
| 41 | The calls to the access service that are in flight | The calls that are in flight are **shared**: a burst of requests for the same decision asks the service once. The cache of the decisions is empty every time its lifetime is over, and the lifetime is short — five seconds by default — so a burst that arrives on the boundary of the lifetime asked the service as many times as there were requests. The service that decides the access of the callers is not a cache that is warmed once, and a stampede on it is an outage of the deployment. The decision that the call answers is the one every waiter of the call receives, and the call is released when it ends. | resolved | `src/domains/workspace-access/client.ts`, `src/domains/workspace-access/workspace-access.test.ts` |
| 42 | The rewrite of the path | A route may declare the path its upstream receives. The prefix of the route was part of the path of the request, so an application that is mounted somewhere else had to know the prefix of the gateway — every upstream had to be built around the shape of the urls of the gateway. The rewrite of a route is a list of segments: the placeholders of the path of the route are the values the caller sent, and `{rest}` is the part of the path that follows the prefix the route matched. A route that does not declare one forwards the path of the request, and the behaviour of a deployment that declares none does not change. The values are the ones the caller sent, so the encoding of the path is not touched, and the signature of the identity covers the path as the upstream receives it. | resolved | `src/domains/routing/match.ts`, `src/domains/configuration/schema.ts`, `test/contract/rewrite.test.ts` |
| 43 | The trace of a request | The gateway writes a `traceparent` header of its own to the upstream: the trace context of the W3C. The header of the caller is not forwarded as it is — a malformed one would confuse the collector of the upstream — and the span of the gateway is new, so the span of the upstream is a child of it and the trace of the caller continues. The id of the trace is written in the log of the request next to the request id, so the two halves of a request are found with the same search. The latency of the upstream is measured on its own and is published as `shuttle_upstream_duration_seconds`: it is the time from the moment the request left the gateway to the final headers of the answer, and it is not the latency of the gateway, which also holds the authentication, the limits and the access service. | resolved | `src/domains/forwarding/trace.ts`, `src/domains/proxying/exchange.ts`, `test/contract/trace.test.ts` |
| 44 | The upstreams of a route | A route may name several upstreams and the gateway tries them in the order of the list. The next one is tried when the **connection** to the first one fails, and only when the request may be sent again: a request that changes the state of an upstream may not be sent twice, and a request that has a body cannot be replayed because the bytes are already on their way to the first upstream. The budget of the answer is the budget of the whole exchange, so a list of upstreams does not make the timeout of a route longer. The handshake of a websocket goes to the first upstream: a handshake is not a request that may be sent twice. There is no health check: the order of the list is the order of the file, so the behaviour of a route does not depend on anything else. | resolved | `src/domains/proxying/exchange.ts`, `src/domains/configuration/schema.ts`, `test/contract/upstreams.test.ts` |
| 45 | The protocol of the access service | The protocol of the access service is **published**: the request, the answer, the statuses and their meanings are in the specification (§6.1), and the protocol is checked by a **conformance suite** that any implementation may run (`test/integration/access-conformance.ts`). The service is a dependency of the deployment and not a part of Shuttle, so the deployment that writes its own service needs a way to know that it speaks the protocol: a contract that nobody can check is a contract that drifts. A **reference implementation** ships with the project (`services/access-service.ts`): it applies a policy file, it runs in a production environment, and the suite runs against it, so the protocol and its reference do not drift apart either. The reference is not a fixture: the fixtures carry accounts with known passwords and refuse to run in production, and the reference carries the policy of the operator. | resolved | `docs/spec.md` §6.1, `test/integration/access-conformance.ts`, `services/access-service.ts` |
| 46 | The policy that is running | The gateway writes the **policy it is running** in its log, once per generation: one line for the configuration, one line for every route and one warning for every setting that is permissive. A route table is a JSON file and an environment with dozens of variables, and the policy a route really runs with is spread over the two. An operator that has to read the two to find out that a route is open to unauthenticated callers is an operator that will not find out. The warnings are not errors: a deployment may want a route that accepts an unauthenticated caller, or a route that hands the credential of the caller to its upstream. They are settings that look active but that nobody may have read, and a warning that says so is worth more than a silent default. The list of the warnings is short on purpose: a warning that is raised for everything is a warning nobody reads. | resolved | `src/domains/configuration/describe.ts`, `src/runtime/policy-log.ts`, `src/runtime/policy-log.test.ts` |
| 47 | The upstreams of a route that do not answer | An attempt to an upstream that has another upstream behind it is abandoned after `upstreamAttemptMs` (`SHUTTLE_UPSTREAM_ATTEMPT_TIMEOUT_MS`, 2 s by default), and the next upstream is tried. Without it, an upstream that accepts the connection and never answers eats the whole budget of the route — the budget of the answer is the budget of the **whole** exchange — and the upstreams behind it are never reached: the failover of a route only worked for an upstream whose port is closed. The budget of an attempt applies only to a request that may be sent twice, and never to the last upstream of the list, which has no successor. The budget of the answer still bounds the whole exchange, so a list of upstreams never makes a route slower. The runtime cannot tell a connection that was never established from an upstream that is slow, so the budget of an attempt is the answer to a question the gateway cannot ask precisely — and it is only applied where a second request is harmless. | resolved | `src/domains/proxying/exchange.ts`, `test/contract/upstreams.test.ts` |
| 48 | The body that may be sent again | A body is held in memory when it announced a length that fits in `replayMaxBytes` (`SHUTTLE_REPLAY_MAX_BYTES`, 64 KiB by default), so that the request may be sent to a second upstream when the first one fails. Before this, a request with a body was never sent twice, whatever the method: the failover of a route was therefore useless for the traffic that carries a body, which is most of an API. The body is only read into memory when it announced a length that fits, so the memory of the gateway is bounded by the limit and not by what a client decides to send; a body that does not fit is streamed and the request is sent once, as before. The body is only held in memory when the route names **several** upstreams: a route with one upstream has nothing to fail over to, and streaming its body preserves the early answer of the upstream (§11.2 of the specification). A request that changes the state of an upstream is never sent twice, whatever the body: the method decides, and only the idempotent ones may be sent again. | resolved | `src/domains/proxying/exchange.ts`, `src/domains/proxying/exchange.test.ts`, `test/contract/upstreams.test.ts` |
| 49 | The methods of a route | A route may name the methods it accepts. The matcher is a host and a path, and the method decided between two routes that matched the same request: the route that names the method of the request wins, and the route that names no method is the fallback of the others. Two routes that name different methods are not ambiguous, so a `GET` and a `POST` of the same path may reach two different upstreams; two routes that name the same methods are refused, as before. The rule is deliberately simple and documented: a header-based or a query-based match would make the answer of the gateway depend on a value the caller chooses, and the ambiguity of such a table cannot be checked at load time. | resolved | `src/domains/routing/match.ts`, `src/domains/routing/routing.test.ts`, `test/contract/methods.test.ts` |
| 50 | The grace of an outage of the access service | An outage of the access service took the whole deployment down: the gateway answers `503 access_unavailable` to every request of a route that needs a decision, and the decisions it holds are good for five seconds. A route may ask for a **grace** (`accessStaleGraceMs`, `SHUTTLE_ACCESS_STALE_GRACE_MS`, `0` by default): the last decision of the service is served for that long after the service became unreachable. The grace is **off by default**, because an unreachable service is an outage and the outage has to be visible. A stale decision is served only when it **allows** the caller: a denial that is served instead of the outage would hide the outage from the operator, and the refusal of the caller is the same answer as the refusal of a gateway that fails closed. A decision that is asked for **fresh** is never stale, so the revalidation of an open connection closes a connection whose access was revoked even while the service is down. The grace is a delay of a revocation, and the lifetime of the decisions is the other knob of that delay. | resolved | `src/domains/workspace-access/client.ts`, `src/domains/workspace-access/workspace-access.test.ts`, `test/contract/access.test.ts` |
| 51 | A route that names no workspace | The access service was only asked about a **workspace**, so a deployment that has no workspaces had no way to ask it anything: it had to invent a workspace to get an answer, and the service that decides had to understand a fiction. A route may now ask for a check with `accessCheck`, and the request of the service then carries the subject, the route and the roles, and no workspace. The `workspaceId` of the protocol became optional, the reference implementation decides on the `routes` of its policy file when the request names none, and the conformance suite checks the request that names none. | 53 | The compatibility of the release | The gateway is in the path of every request of the applications behind it, and the deployments write their own access service and their own upstreams against it. | 54 | The health of the upstreams | The failover of a route only reached the second upstream when the request could be **sent again**, so the traffic that matters most — the `POST` of an API — had no failover at all: the request burned the whole budget of the route and the second upstream was never reached.
