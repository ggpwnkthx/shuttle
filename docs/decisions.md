# The decisions of the release

[spec.md](spec.md) is normative for the behavior. This file records the
decisions behind it: what was decided, why, and where the evidence is. A
decision that changes the contract is recorded in the spec and in the
changelog, never only in the code.

The decisions that shape the contract are in [spec.md](spec.md) §15; the
two lists are numbered independently.

| # | Question | Decision | Status | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The supported runtime versions | The gateway is built, tested and released on **Bun 1.4.3**; the contract states **Bun ≥ 1.4.3**. The 1.2 line was measured (1.2.0 and 1.2.23): it does not expose the early answer while the upload runs, it does not cancel the producer of the body, the `ws` client cannot observe the refusal of an upstream handshake, and the upgrade of the caller with the headers of the gateway is refused. | resolved | [runtime.md](runtime.md), `test/network/transport.test.ts` |
| 2 | The upload and the response commitment boundaries | The observable events are the **end of the request stream**, the **final response headers** and the **first byte of the body**. The acceptance of the bytes by the peer is not observable and is not claimed. A failure after the headers are sent ends the body instead of replacing the answer. | resolved | `test/network/transport.test.ts` |
| 3 | The refusal of an upstream WebSocket handshake | It is reported apart from a network failure: the caller receives `502 bad_gateway`, the body of the upstream is not forwarded, the status of the refusal is in the log, and the slot of the connection is released. | resolved | `test/network/transport.test.ts` |
| 4 | The establishment of the connection during the upload | No extra ceiling is added. The upload is governed by the idle `bodyMs` budget only: a client that keeps making progress is never cut off by a total budget. The establishment of the connection to the upstream is part of the `responseMs` budget. | resolved | `test/network/transport.test.ts` |
| 5 | The `Authorization` header of the caller and the standard `Forwarded` header | The standard `forwarded` header is **removed** in both directions. The `Authorization` header of the caller is **forwarded** to the upstream unchanged: the upstream is an application of the same platform that the operator chose, and the identity of the caller travels in the headers of the gateway and in the signature. | resolved | `src/domains/forwarding/headers.test.ts` |
| 6 | The representation of the headers | The roles are written as a comma separated list. A role that contains a comma or a control character is dropped from the header and stays in the request to the access service. The identity values are truncated to 512 characters and the control characters are removed. The name of a cookie is case sensitive. | resolved | `src/domains/forwarding/identity-signature.test.ts`, `src/domains/forwarding/headers.test.ts` |
| 7 | The purpose of a token | The provider does not publish a token type claim, so the gateway validates the signature, the issuer, the audience, the expiry and the subject, and nothing else. The algorithms are pinned and the tolerance of the clock is five seconds. A deployment whose provider publishes a token type claim adds the check in the authentication domain; the ports make it a local change. | resolved | `test/integration/oidc.test.ts` |
| 8 | The consumption of the login transaction | The state of the login is single use on four levels: the state cookie is deleted as soon as the callback is processed, the state of the callback has to be the state of the cookie, the verifier and the nonce bind the exchange to the login attempt, and the authorization code is single use at the provider. Two replicas that process the same callback at the same time both reach the provider, and the provider refuses the second exchange. | resolved | `src/domains/authentication/principal.test.ts` |
| 9 | The outage of the cache of the decisions | Only an explicit decision of the access service is cached. A transport failure is never cached. When the cache is unavailable the gateway asks the service again and denies when that fails. | resolved | `src/domains/workspace-access/workspace-access.test.ts`, `test/integration/access.test.ts` |
| 10 | The live authorization of a WebSocket | The connection is authorized at the handshake **and** revalidated every `SHUTTLE_WS_REVALIDATE_INTERVAL_MS`. The revalidation checks the route, the session and the access, asks the access service again instead of reading the cache, and closes the connection with the code `1008` when one of them fails. A revalidation that cannot be made closes the connection: a connection the gateway cannot vouch for is a connection it must not keep. | resolved | `src/domains/websockets/bridge.test.ts`, `test/contract/websocket-revocation.test.ts` |
| 11 | The provisioning of the verifier of the signature | The upstream is given the **derived** key, not the master secret. The key is versioned by the version of the signature (`v3`). | resolved | `src/domains/forwarding/identity-signature.test.ts` |
| 12 | The external host and the caching of the responses | The gateway does not cache a response and does not add cache headers. The answers of the upstreams are forwarded as they are, including their cache headers: the gateway is not the owner of the policy of the caching. | resolved | `test/contract/endpoints.test.ts` |
| 13 | The topology of the replicas and the TLS of Redis | The replicas share the sessions, the rate limits and the cache of the decisions through Redis. The TLS of the endpoint is verified with the certificate authority of the deployment. The caps of a process (sessions, connections, buffers, exchanges) are per process: a deployment with several replicas multiplies them. | resolved | [operations.md](operations.md), `src/runtime/env.ts` |
| 14 | The drain policy | During the drain the readiness probe answers `503` with the body `draining`, the listener stops accepting new work, the WebSocket connections are closed with the code 1001, and the exchanges that are running are cancelled when the deadline is reached. | resolved | `test/contract/lifecycle.test.ts` |
| 15 | The host of a request | The **effective host** is the `x-forwarded-host` of a trusted proxy and the `Host` header otherwise. It decides the route, the origin the browser is compared with, and the value the signature covers. The header of a client that is not a trusted proxy is ignored, and a value that is not a plausible host is ignored. The last value of the list is used, because a proxy that appends writes its own value last. | resolved | `test/contract/forwarding.test.ts` |
| 16 | The metrics of the requests | Every answer of the gateway is counted, the refusals included, with the route it was matched on or `unmatched`. The labels are bounded: `other` for a method or a status that is not tracked, so neither a client nor an upstream can grow the number of series without limit. The route is capped at 500, with the rest aggregated into `overflow`. | resolved | `test/contract/metrics.test.ts`, `src/observability/observability.test.ts` |
| 17 | The scopes of the limits | Four scopes: the deployment, the caller over all the routes, the caller on one route, and the authenticated caller on one route. A scope keyed by an **address** cannot bound a flood that spreads over many addresses, and it cannot tell two tenants behind the same address apart. The scope of the deployment has no address in it; the scope of a principal is keyed by the subject. | resolved | `src/domains/rate-limiting/rate-limiting.test.ts` |
| 18 | The table of the limiter of the memory | The table of the windows is bounded at 10 000 keys, and a key leaves it when its window is over, **never** because another key needed its place. A limiter that evicts a live window hands the evicted caller a fresh budget, so a caller that sprays addresses resets its own counter at will. When the table is full, the requests that do not fit are counted in one shared window, so the flood is bounded by the same limit. | resolved | `src/domains/rate-limiting/rate-limiting.test.ts` |
| 19 | The number of exchanges at the same time | The exchanges are bounded by `SHUTTLE_MAX_CONCURRENT_REQUESTS`. A client that opens a request and never sends its body holds a connection until the idle budget of the upload runs out, which is five minutes by default; without a cap, that is enough to exhaust the resources of the process. An exchange that does not fit is refused with `503 gateway_overloaded` rather than queued. | resolved | `src/domains/proxying/exchange.test.ts` |
| 20 | The rate limits of a production deployment | The gateway refuses to start in production when every limit is disabled, unless `SHUTTLE_ALLOW_NO_RATE_LIMITS=true`. The limiter is the only protection of the gateway against a flood, and a setting that looks active but is not a security hole. | resolved | `src/runtime/env.test.ts` |
| 21 | The revocation of an access | The latency of a revocation is the TTL of the cache of the decisions (`SHUTTLE_ACCESS_CACHE_TTL_MS`, 5 s by default). It is the knob an operator turns when a revocation has to be immediate. The revalidation of a WebSocket asks the service again and does not read the cache. | resolved | `src/domains/workspace-access/workspace-access.test.ts` |
| 22 | The state of a production deployment | The state is shared: the sessions and the state of the replicas live in a Redis server, or the deployment says explicitly that it is a single node that keeps them in the process (`SHUTTLE_ALLOW_MEMORY_STATE=true`). A session in the memory of one replica is unknown to the others, so the callers are logged out at random, and a limit in the memory of one replica is the limit of that replica and not the limit of the deployment, so a flood is bounded by the number of the replicas. | resolved | `src/runtime/env.test.ts` |
